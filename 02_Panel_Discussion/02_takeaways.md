- Nomura Asset Management uses bond frameworks to compare what issuers actually finance. For two utilities with green bonds, Jason Mortimer distinguished renewable generation from a green headquarters by asking which project changes the utility’s core business and delivers more impact per dollar.
- For transition projects, Nomura wants the expected operating life and the emissions that would arise under business as usual. Bloomberg could capture both alongside projected emissions so investors can estimate lifetime savings and judge how long a project might lock in fossil fuel use.
- Nomura tracks whether an issuer’s capital spending follows its transition plan over time. S&P Global Ratings assesses what the issuer is likely to achieve in the next five to seven years when judging the credibility of its longer-term commitments.
- Nomura reported that spreads on its European utility holdings with renewable generation widened less than those of more fossil-fuel-dependent issuers during the 2022 gas shock. Mortimer attributed that experience to the utilities’ generation mix and low operating costs.
- Tokio Marine & Nichido bought Yokohama’s entire ¥1.5 billion flood resilience bond after identifying the three mitigation projects and estimating the flood damage and insurance claims they could avert. Bloomberg could link bonds to project locations, baseline losses and disclosed modelling assumptions so investors can compare estimates on consistent terms.
- Tokio Marine & Nichido reported orders of about 1.7 times the ¥200 billion of green and nature bonds it issued for its headquarters. The company explained how purchasing Japanese timber could support harvesting and replanting, giving investors a concrete rationale for the building’s nature component.
- The Islamic Development Bank said its October 2025 sukuk was five times oversubscribed and attracted first-time sukuk investors. The bank has added climate adaptation and food security to the activities eligible for financing under its sustainable finance framework.
- The Islamic Development Bank identified two barriers to financing biodiversity and adaptation projects. Long-term public benefits can be hard to turn into project revenue, and the lack of common impact measures makes it difficult for investors to track performance.
- S&P Global Ratings looks for a baseline, measurable water outcomes and adverse effects when reviewing blue bonds. Desalination can protect water supplies while raising emissions, and aquaculture can ease fishing pressure while damaging habitat.
- Jason Mortimer favoured engaging with data-centre projects to improve resource efficiency, while Yoshinari Endo said insurers also need to know whether a facility can keep operating if a catastrophe or equipment failure disrupts its concentrated power, computing and data assets. Bloomberg could link financed facilities to disclosed power and water use and business-continuity measures so bond investors can assess operating risks alongside environmental performance.
