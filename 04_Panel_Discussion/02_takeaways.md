- Moody’s Ratings estimated that about US$5 trillion of rated debt globally has elevated exposure to climate transition risk, compared with about US$10 billion in labelled transition bonds. Rahul Ghosh acknowledged that other instruments finance transition, so the two figures illustrate the scale of the exposure without measuring the entire financing shortfall.
- BNP Paribas’s Ling Han said at least three Korean utilities had taken their transition plans to international investors through US dollar issuance. Investors welcomed the dialogue while questioning how projects aligned with standards and whether they risked locking in carbon-intensive assets.
- In a BNP Paribas survey of more than 100 investors, respondents considered an issuer’s interim targets, controversies and planned capital spending when assessing its transition. Bloomberg could link bond frameworks to issuer targets and disclosed capital expenditure so investors can compare the funding behind competing plans.
- JFE Steel said defined uses of proceeds and a credible transition plan attracted investors reluctant to provide conventional financing to high-emitting steelmakers. The issuer saw a broader funding base, while better borrowing terms were less certain.
- JFE Steel reports allocations and environmental benefits annually and explains when changes in power supply, technology or demand for green steel alter its timetable. Bloomberg could retain dated revisions and the issuer’s reasons so credit analysts can distinguish a postponed milestone from a change in the long-term goal.
- Moody’s saw sustainability value in a diesel public transport project in a part of Southeast Asia that had no public transport. Ghosh said the same investment would warrant a different assessment in central Madrid, illustrating why the available local alternative matters when comparing projects.
- BNP Paribas said issuers can interpret a lower second-party opinion score as evidence of a weak transition framework. Moody’s argued that an opinion should reflect the activity being financed and that issuers can address greenwashing concerns by explaining their choices and the alternatives they considered.
- Moody’s looks for returns from cost savings or new revenues, access to critical inputs and evidence that management can deliver through policy or credit cycles. Ghosh cited retirement dates for existing infrastructure as one sign that investors can test against an issuer’s plan.
- Amundi’s Frank Tsui said only a handful of Asian commercial investors he had spoken with had a transition framework they could use in an investment proposal. He saw interest in the market, but investors still needed a way to fit transition into their asset allocations.
- Amundi described a roughly €3 billion blended finance initiative for emerging-market green bonds with underlying holdings averaging around BBB. Tsui said catalytic capital helped the fund obtain an A2 rating, making the exposure accessible to insurers whose mandates restrict direct purchases of unrated or lower-rated bonds.
