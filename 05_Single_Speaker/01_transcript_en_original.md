# Guest speech · Kazuto Uchida, President of the Government Pension Investment Fund

[Official conference agenda](https://www.icmagroup.org/events/9th-annual-icma-and-jsda-sustainable-bond-conference/).

Good afternoon, ladies and gentlemen. My name is Kazuto Uchida, president of the Government Pension Investment Fund (GPIF). It is an honour to be here. I am grateful for the opportunity to deliver this speech at the ninth annual Sustainable Bond Conference of the International Capital Market Association (ICMA) and the Japan Securities Dealers Association. I would like to begin by expressing my sincere gratitude and respect to ICMA. Many companies around the world, including in Japan, issue bonds in line with international standards based on ICMA’s principles. I believe issuing these bonds makes a tangible contribution to these companies’ [unclear].

As I mentioned last year, GPIF formulated its Sustainability Investment Policy. This policy builds on our previous approach to environmental, social and governance (ESG) investment. It rests on two pillars. The first is to mitigate sustainability-related risks and enhance the sustainability of markets. The second is to secure the market-average return. By balancing these objectives, we seek to improve the long-term investment return of our entire portfolio.

Sustainability investment encompasses more than the allocation of capital to ESG indexes or funds. It includes stewardship activities, sustainability-related risk analysis, dialogue and collaboration with relevant organisations, and sharing information on sustainability. These efforts contribute to long-term corporate value and sustainable growth in the market. GPIF works with various participants in the investment chain. By combining these initiatives and engaging across that chain, from asset managers to investee companies, we aim to contribute to the sustainable growth of those companies and of the market as a whole.

In light of the revision to Japan’s Stewardship Code, GPIF has updated its policy for fulfilling its stewardship responsibilities and expanded its activities. We have established principles for the exercise of voting rights. We encourage our external asset managers to engage with investee companies and issuers, take sustainability factors such as ESG into account, and exercise voting rights appropriately. Through these stewardship efforts, including engagement by our asset managers, we aim to foster a virtuous cycle across the investment chain. Engagement can enhance long-term corporate value, which supports growth in the capital markets and the broader economy and ultimately leads to greater long-term investment returns.

Previously, we worked primarily with our domestic equity asset managers to highlight excellent company disclosures in four categories, including high-quality integrated reports. In fiscal 2025, we brought these together in a single framework for assessing sustainability disclosure, with materiality as the main selection criterion. This process identified 89 companies with excellent sustainability disclosures and 66 whose disclosures showed significant improvement.

The chart breaks down the selected companies by market capitalisation. It shows comparable representation among large- and mid-cap companies. This suggests that management practices informed by materiality are becoming more widespread among Japanese companies.

The International Sustainability Standards Board has developed global sustainability disclosure standards focused on financial materiality. In Japan, reporting based on the Sustainability Standards Board of Japan standards, which are consistent with the international standards, will be phased in for listed companies starting with the fiscal year ending in March 2027. I expect this to make information more comparable across companies globally. [unclear]

Finally, I would like to discuss our dialogue and collaboration with other organisations. To advance sustainability investment within applicable laws and regulations, it is essential to engage with other asset owners in Japan and abroad, international initiatives and business associations. In Japan, we hold the Keidanren–GPIF Asset Owners’ Roundtable several times a year to exchange views on dialogue with asset managers, the exercise of voting rights and engagement with ESG rating agencies. Internationally, we participate in networks such as the Principles for Responsible Investment and the International Corporate Governance Network, where we give speeches, take part in discussions and share GPIF’s philosophy and initiatives.

In closing, I hope ICMA’s efforts will encourage further issuance of sustainability-related bonds and contribute to a deeper, more liquid market. I look forward to ICMA continuing to work with other stakeholders to update global best practices and improve the transparency, effectiveness and reliability of disclosures. As a universal owner with a large portfolio of pension assets invested globally, GPIF remains committed to advancing sustainability investment in Japan and internationally. That concludes my remarks. Thank you very much.
