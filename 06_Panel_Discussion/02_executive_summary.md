Investors can see what a resilience bond pays for more easily than they can judge the losses it prevents. In the panel on labelled bonds and disaster resilience in Asia, Tokyo’s Sadaaki Shimizu said investors responded well to a film showing how underground reservoirs protect homes, while underwriter Daisaku Chabata stressed that any impact estimate depends on which event is assumed and who would be exposed. Philippine SEC assistant director Grace S. Ayson described the financing consequence: private capital is difficult to attract when adaptation returns take years and local governments lack the technical resources to prepare bankable projects. Bloomberg could link project allocations to local hazards, indicators and scenario assumptions so investors can distinguish reported outcomes from estimates of avoided damage.
