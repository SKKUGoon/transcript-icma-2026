Wrap of the Tokyo Conference

1. Transition Finance

Transition finance was the dominant theme, with a major focus on moving from discussion to practical implementation, especially for hard-to-abate sectors.
New ICMA Guidelines: The conference marked the official launch of the Climate Transition Bond Guidelines. This creates a new, dedicated label (distinct from Green Bonds) to help finance credible decarbonization projects in high-emission sectors like steel, cement, and shipping.
Japan's National Strategy: Japan's leadership was a central backdrop. Officials highlighted the national "GX" (Green Transformation) strategy, aiming to mobilize 150 trillion JPY, and the 2024 launch of the world's first sovereign transition bond. 
Practical Application: A panel discussion explored how to finance these transitions. Key points included:
Financing "brown-to-green" projects in sectors like electric steel and nuclear power.
The need for investors to have a 3-layer approach: portfolio-level targets, investment in both high-emitters and the tech solutions they need, and system-level engagement.
Second-Party Opinion could assess the alignment with the new guidelines to give banks and issuers confidence.


*Details of Climate Transition Bond Guidelines (CTBG):
Introduces CTB Label: A new standalone "Climate Transition Bond" (CTB) label is introduced for use-of-proceeds bonds to fund key decarbonization projects.
Aligns with the Paris Agreement goals and complements the Climate Transition Finance Handbook (CTFH), which is the entity level guidance.
Provides guidance on eligible transition projects with a definition and safeguards, as well as a preliminary and non-exhaustive list of CT project categories.
Aims to help finance the transition of fossil-fuel and other hard-to-abate sectors, which have struggled to use existing sustainable bond markets.
Attaches existing official sector and market-based taxonomies to help issuers identify the relevant sources
Same as GSS bonds, CTBs must also still follow the four core components: Use of Proceeds, Project Selection, Management of Proceeds, and Reporting. 
High-emission issuers using Sustainability-Linked Bonds (SLBs) should use KPIs focused on GHG reduction and set ambitious, science-based targets.
Designed as a dynamic framework, evolving with market feedback, regulations, and emerging methodologies.


2. Nature & Biodiversity Finance

A second major market focus was the need to integrate nature and biodiversity into sustainable finance which was discussed in the conference.
New ICMA Guidance: ICMA recently published the Sustainable Bonds for Nature (Practitioner's Guide). This is not a new label but a framework to help issuers integrate nature-positive and biodiversity-focused projects (like watershed restoration or sustainable agriculture) into the existing Green Bond Principles.
Focus on Measurement: Panelists stressed moving beyond simple conservation to finance measurable, scalable nature-based solutions. This includes leveraging science-based tools and the expertise of the conservation community.
Social Co-Benefits: A key discussion point was that nature projects cannot succeed without deep engagement with Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities (IPLCs).
Blue Finance: There was a strong call to action to scale the Blue Bond market to finance the Ocean Economy.

3. Data, Disclosure, and Market Credibility

Building trust and proving real-world impact was a foundational theme. The discussions showed a clear market-wide shift in focus: moving from high-level ambition to concrete, evidence-backed implementation.
Demand for Post-Issuance Data: To combat greenwashing risks, investors and regulators are demanding more robust post-issuance tracking. Panels emphasized that transparent reporting on how proceeds are used, the progress of financed projects, and the verification of their "real impact" is essential for market integrity.
The "Say-Do" Gap: A key statistic highlighted at the conference underscored the challenge that while a lot of companies now have climate plans, only 11% disclose having the capital expenditure aligned to actually fund them. This gap between pledges and tangible financial planning is a major credibility concern for investors.

4. The Role of Regulators and Public Institutions

The role of the official sector is expanding from guidance to active market participation, with central banks now defining climate change as a core mandate linked to price and financial stability. National regulators are fostering regional cooperation to develop "realistic" and "on-ground" investable projects, while market infrastructure bodies are building an operational backbone with tools like green bond collateral services to improve liquidity. Simultaneously, international organizations and development banks are focused on unlocking private capital by promoting blended finance models and facilitating high-level discussions to solve complex issues like carbon lock-in.

5. Market Trends & Specific Instruments

Discussions also focused on the evolution and performance of specific bond types.
Sustainability-Linked Bonds: A dedicated breakout session addressed the development of SLB market. Panelists identified key reasons for the contraction of SLB issuance this year: Issuer hesitation over setting ambitious targets, especially for Scope 3 ;  Strong investor preference for UoP instruments and challenges with penalty structures. The panelists called for more innovation to the SLB market in target setting.
Social and Resilience Bonds: A panel on social bonds featured the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's "Resilience Bonds." These bonds finance climate adaptation and disaster resilience (e.g., flood and earthquake defenses), demonstrating a growing intersection between social and environmental goals.


Takeaways:

Transition Moves from Abstract to Actionable: The new ICMA Climate Transition Bond Guidelines (CTBG) provides the "hands-on" bond-level framework. This moves transition finance to a more practical implementation, with market participants now forecasting a significant increase in issuance, especially from Asia's hard-to-abate sectors.
Methodology Review for "Transition" Label:  Our current "dual-tagging" (e.g., flagging the Japan sovereign climate transition bond as both "Green" and "Transition") could be re-evaluated. We need to conduct a review in the market to determine our potential product update.  We are already receiving client inquiries asking if we will change our methodology for Transition Bonds following the new CTBG. 
Taxonomy Alignment Data: With more ICMA guidelines and regional regulations emerging, tracking taxonomy and standards alignment will become a critical client need.
"Nature" as a Major Theme: The launch of the "Bonds to Finance Nature" guide and the strong calls to scale the Blue Bond market are clear signals. Investors are rapidly moving to price in biodiversity and nature-related risks and opportunities.
Demand for "Real-World" Impact Data: The market is demanding data to evaluate and assess real-world decarbonization impact. This presents a clear and significant opportunity for our Impact and Allocation data products.
