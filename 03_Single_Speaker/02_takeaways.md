- Sadanori Ito of Japan’s Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry described the country’s GX Economy Transition Bonds as the foundation of its green transformation policy. He credited the securities industry with helping issuance proceed smoothly and said the government intends to continue using proceeds for up-front investment support.
- Japan’s emissions trading system is in its company assessment stage, Ito said. He expects full-scale allowance allocation and trading to begin in autumn 2027 and asked the domestic securities industry for its cooperation.
- Ito said Japan’s sixth Strategic Energy Plan anticipated falling electricity demand as the population shrank. The seventh plan reversed that outlook as AI and data centres increased projected demand, making power supply a factor in national competitiveness.
- Japan’s GX strategic regions programme pairs large data centres with carbon-free power in nine areas from Hokkaido to Kyushu, Ito said. Bloomberg could link disclosed project locations and power sources to bond allocations so investors can identify how funded data centres expect to meet their electricity needs.
- Japan imports more than 90% of its crude oil from the Middle East, Ito said. He distinguished legislation under consideration to reward procurement via routes outside the Strait of Hormuz from efforts already under way to strengthen naphtha reserves.
- Ito said Japan plans to publish a Power GX Declaration by year-end with more detail on renewable and nuclear energy measures. Its August outline also addresses the security of fossil fuel supplies while Japan continues to need them.
- Japan intends to support priority industries from technology development through capital investment to the creation of demand, Ito said. He cited perovskite solar cells, next-generation geothermal power and green chemicals as examples of the technologies and industries covered.
- Ito said local authorities can draw on industrial complexes built during Japan’s high-growth years as well as local carbon-free power sources. The GX strategic regions programme lets them plan investment across a whole area.
- Ito saw South Korea’s K-GX strategy and Europe’s increased emphasis on industrial competitiveness alongside carbon neutrality as signs of convergence. In his view, more countries are trying to reconcile economic growth and energy security with emissions cuts.
- Ito counted more than 500 projects under the Asia Zero Emission Community framework and said Japanese companies were already using it to pursue energy transition projects in Asia. He sees a role for financial institutions outside Japan as those projects develop.
