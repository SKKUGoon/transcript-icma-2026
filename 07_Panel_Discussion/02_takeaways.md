- Zenkyoren assesses yen credit investments against continuity, economic and social return, and transparency. Shiko Higashi said showing what an investment achieved helps the cooperative insurer justify putting more capital to work when the issuer returns.
- Zenkyoren has moved from financing individual agricultural initiatives to examining six stages of the value chain. Higashi said the team identifies a problem at each stage, looks for companies offering solutions, and approaches them about bond investment.
- Tokyu Land’s progress on Scope 1 and 2 emissions made its earlier target insufficiently ambitious for a new sustainability-linked bond, according to Nomura’s Shun Machiyama. The issuer turned to a Scope 3 reduction target that requires action across its supply chain.
- Tokyu Land paired its Scope 3 indicator with a target of 3,000 hectares of environmental conservation area by 2030. Machiyama cited the climate and nature-linked issues as an example of a Japanese issuer using ICMA’s nature guidance.
- IFC collects separate evidence on the use and impact of bond proceeds, wider development benefits such as jobs or access to finance, and ongoing environmental and social risk controls. Pushkala Ratan said IFC also helps clients build the capacity to report those results.
- IFC has more confidence in impact data for renewable energy, buildings and electric vehicles than for social projects, transition finance or resilience. Ratan said the data constraints are particularly acute for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises.
- CDP’s Makiko Yamamoto cautioned that ranking companies side by side can obscure differences in what they report. She urged data providers to show where definitions, calculation methods and reporting boundaries agree or differ, so a company’s disclosure can be reused.
- Co-investors can send separate questionnaires on the same transaction, leaving the person compiling the data to answer four or five requests a year. Ratan called for investors to agree on what they ask issuers to supply.
- Zenkyoren can receive ostensibly identical indicators with different units, formulas, levels of detail or measurement periods. Higashi said his team discusses data availability with the issuer before investing so that it knows what results it can track.
- Bloomberg could retain original indicators, units and baselines alongside normalized measures so bond investors can distinguish genuine progress toward a target from a change in how the issuer measures it.
