- The IFC representative said emerging-market issuers increasingly recognize that impact reporting gives transactions credibility. In the representative’s experience, that conversation has changed over the past five or six years, at different speeds across markets.
- IFC collects three kinds of evidence on its investments. A green bond’s use of proceeds and environmental results answer a different question from the jobs it helps create or whether environmental and social safeguards remain in place.
- IFC couples its requests for impact data with technical assistance on strategy and reporting. The representative said the capacity to produce data is a particularly acute constraint for micro, small and medium-sized enterprises.
- IFC has more confidence in impact data for renewable energy, buildings and electric vehicles than for social projects, transition finance or adaptation and resilience. Its experience points to differences in coverage by theme even within the same market.
- Similar investments can report different indicators, making direct comparison difficult. The IFC representative said harmonizing eligibility standards has advanced further than harmonizing the metrics used to assess outcomes.
- IFC’s social bond framework focuses on practical measures it encounters repeatedly, including farmers gaining access to finance and people with mobile connections. Financial intermediaries and asset managers may lack the underlying beneficiary data needed for more detailed measures.
- The IFC representative said smaller loans to farmers and businesses call for a different reporting approach from large corporate or renewable energy financings. The size of each loan affects how much information it is practical to collect.
- Co-investors can send separate questionnaires for the same transaction, leaving the person compiling the data to answer four or five requests in a year. The IFC representative called for investors to agree on what they ask issuers to supply.
- The IFC representative endorsed another panelist’s point about interoperability. She saw scope to reuse data already collected for portfolio decisions and to carry that approach into newer areas such as resilience.
- Bloomberg could retain issuer-reported indicators, units and definitions alongside mapped metrics so bond investors can compare similar projects and identify cases where differences in measurement prevent a meaningful comparison.
